FAQ

How accurate is it?

A phone camera is a good meter, not a lab instrument. With the automatic profile the expected error is about ±1/2 stop (±25% lux). After you calibrate against your own lux meter or camera, about ±1/3 stop (±10%). The app always shows its expected error next to the reading.

How do I calibrate?

Settings → Calibration. Put the phone next to your lux meter, screen up, so both see the same light, or compare an exposure reading with your camera's built-in meter, then enter what yours shows and tap Apply correction. Outdoors in direct midday sun you can also run the Sunny 16 check: expect about EV 15 at ISO 100.

Spot or average, and why does the reading change when I move?

Spot meters the small circle; average meters the whole frame, centre-weighted. Long-press the circle to switch. A spot meter reacts to what is inside the circle, so moving from a bright wall to a dark doorway changes the reading by several stops. Point at one surface, hold still for a second, then read or tap Hold.

Why do I lock one of aperture and shutter?

Light is one number (EV); aperture and shutter are two settings that can produce it. You choose the one that matters for the picture (aperture for depth of field, shutter for motion) and the app meters the other. The "Same exposure" row lists every pair that gives the same exposure; tap one to use it.

Which camera for lux?

Lux mode uses the front camera by default: lay the phone flat, screen up, where the light lands. Switch to the rear camera to measure the light on a surface you point at.

What does Pro include and how do I restore it?

Pro is a one-time purchase, no subscription: unlimited cameras, films and saved readings, reciprocity correction for long exposures, colour temperature in Lux mode, CSV export, and future Pro features. To restore on a new phone, open Settings → Restore purchases while signed in with the same Apple Account.

Reciprocity correction?

Film loses sensitivity during long exposures (reciprocity failure). For exposures of 1 s and longer, Pro applies the film's published correction and shows the extra stops under the shutter value. Films marked "approximate" use a generic curve.

The reading says "ranging" or "low light".

"Ranging" means the camera is adjusting its exposure to the scene; wait a moment. "Low light" means the scene is near the camera's limit, so expect about ±1/2 stop. Very bright direct light sources (the sun, a bare bulb) can clip and are not meterable; meter a lit surface instead.

Does the app collect any data?

No. No account, no analytics, no ads, no network access. Readings stay on your phone. See the Privacy Policy.
